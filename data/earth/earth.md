# Earth - NASA Science

NASA explores Earth as one dynamic, connected system. Pioneering science, groundbreaking missions, and new technologies reveal our home planet as never before. The trusted science and open data that result empower communities, public agencies, and industries to prepare for hazards, manage vital resources, and plan for the future. What NASA learns about Earth also propels exploration beyond our planet.

NASA sets the standard for discovering how our planet works.

NASA brings scientists and engineers together to build pioneering missions that reveal what we couldn’t see before, from changes in water supplies to atmospheric conditions that affect aviation and public safety. These observations help explain how Earth’s oceans, land, ice, atmosphere, and life affect one another.

Other agencies and businesses put NASA’s discoveries and technologies to work in everyday services. NASA also makes its data available to everyone, giving researchers and industries ranging from farming to insurance a trusted foundation for decisions that affect our economy, security, and natural resources.

Six Decades of Discovery

### NASA's View of Home

From NASA’s first image of Earth in 1959 to today’s detailed views of land, oceans, ice, and atmosphere, see how observing our planet from space has transformed what we know and expanded what is possible.

[**From Earthrise to Today**](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/advancing-earth-observation-at-nasa-since-release-of-earthrise-photo/)

### NISAR

With one of the most powerful radar systems ever launched into space, NISAR maps nearly all of Earth’s land and ice surfaces twice every 12 days, revealing ground movement, ice flow, disaster impacts, and changes across forests, wetlands, and croplands.

[Explore NISAR](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/nisar/)

### PACE

By measuring tiny differences in light, PACE reads the ocean’s swirling colors like fingerprints, revealing microscopic life that supports fisheries while tracking harmful algal blooms, clouds, smoke, dust, and plant health.

[Explore PACE](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/pace/)

### SWOT

SWOT maps Earth's water, measuring the rise and fall of rivers, lakes, reservoirs, and the ocean to improve freshwater management, flood preparedness, and ocean science.

[Explore SWOT](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/SWOT/)
