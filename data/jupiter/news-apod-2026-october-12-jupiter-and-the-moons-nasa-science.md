**Explanation:** How many moons can you see? The best answer is five: four orbiting [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/) and [Luna](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/#h-how-the-moon-got-its-name) orbiting Earth. This multi-moon picture was possible because [Earth's Moon](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2023-november-30-artemis-1-flight-day-13/) moved directly [in front](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2023-august-8-moon-meets-jupiter/) of [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/) last week. The [featured image](https://www.instagram.com/p/DeKFXPOP1y1/) is a series of exposures near alignment taken from [Westchester](https://youtu.be/91YWWW10suA), [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_\(state\)), [USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States). The sunlit crescent of Earth's Moon, on the lower left, is overexposed, while the Moon's night side, on the upper right, is faintly illuminated by [Earthshine](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2025-april-3-the-da-vinci-glow/). Lined up [nearly vertically](https://www.reddit.com/r/aww/comments/b7s5sw/came_home_from_work_the_other_day_to_find_all_5/#lightbox) above our Moon, listed from bottom to top, are Jupiter's bright [Galilean satellites](https://www.nasa.gov/history/410-years-ago-galileo-discovers-jupiters-moons/): Ganymede, Io, Callisto, and Europa. [Callisto](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2025-september-1-callisto-dirty-battered-iceball/), [Ganymede](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2023-november-28-ganymede-from-juno/), and [Io](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2026-january-20-io-in-true-color/) are larger than Earth's Moon, while [Europa](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/europa/) is only slightly smaller. NASA's robotic spacecraft [Juno](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/juno/) is currently orbiting [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2026-january-18-jupiter-from-the-webb-space-telescope/) and has made close passes near three of these moons.
