# APOD: 2025 September 1 - Callisto: Dirty Battered Iceball - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

**Explanation:** Its surface is the most densely cratered in the Solar System -- but what's inside? [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/) 's moon [Callisto](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/callisto/) is a battered ball of dirty ice that is slightly smaller than the planet [Mercury](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/) . It was visited by NASA's [Galileo spacecraft](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/galileo/) in the 1990s and 2000s, but the recently reprocessed [featured image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Callisto_-_July_8_1979_\(38926064465\).jpg) is from a flyby of NASA's [Voyager 2](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-2/) in 1979. The moon would appear darker if it weren't for the tapestry of light-colored [fractured](https://thumbs.dreamstime.com/b/black-cat-pawing-broken-glass-window-s-paw-reaches-its-sharp-claws-visible-shards-scattered-around-creating-334701018.jpg) surface ice created by eons of impacts. The [interior of Callisto](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap981106.html) is potentially even more interesting because therein might lie an internal layer of liquid water. This potential underground sea is a candidate to [harbor life](https://www.numberanalytics.com/blog/astrobiological-potential-of-callisto) -- similar with sister moons [Europa](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap240329.html) and [Ganymede](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap231128.html) . Callisto is larger than [Luna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_\(name\)) , [Earth's Moon](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/) , and even with its high ice content is still more massive. ESA's [JUICE](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Juice) and NASA's [Europa Clipper](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/) missions are now headed [out to Jupiter](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap170523.html) to better investigate its largest moons .
