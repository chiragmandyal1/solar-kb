# APOD: 2025 November 11 - Jupiter in Ultraviolet from Hubble - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

**Explanation:** Jupiter looks a bit different in ultraviolet light. To better interpret [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/) 's cloud motions and to help NASA's robotic [Juno](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/juno/) spacecraft understand the planetary context of the small fields that it sees, the [Hubble Space Telescope](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/about-hubble/) was being directed to [regularly image](https://archive.stsci.edu/proposal_search.php?mission=hst&id=14661) the entire Jovian giant. The colors of Jupiter [being monitored](https://archive.stsci.edu/proposal_search.php?mission=hst&id=14661) go beyond the normal human visual range to include both [ultraviolet](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/10_ultravioletwaves) and (not pictured) [infrared](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap180221.html) light. [Featured](https://www.flickr.com/photos/geckzilla/39448899094/in/photostream/) from 2017, Jupiter appears different in near ultraviolet light, partly because the amount of sunlight reflected back is distinct, giving differing cloud [heights](https://www.reddit.com/r/Awww/comments/1ipizhk/stacked_cats_to_save_space/#lightbox) and latitudes [discrepant brightnesses](https://solarsystem.nasa.gov/resources/109/jupiter-in-blue-ultraviolet-and-near-infrared/) . In the [near UV, Jupiter](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/hubble-provides-unique-ultraviolet-view-of-jupiter/) 's poles appear relatively dark, as does its [Great Red Spot](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap180425.html) and a smaller (optically) [white oval](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap170228.html) to the right. The [String of Pearl](https://astronomynow.com/2017/06/26/jupiters-string-of-pearls/) storms farther to the right, however, are brightest in near ultraviolet, and so here appear (false-color) pink. Jupiter's largest moon [Ganymede](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/ganymede/) appears on the upper left. [Juno](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/jpl/nasas-juno-mission-expands-into-the-future/) continues on a looping 33-day orbit around Jupiter, while Earth-orbiting Hubble is aging and now relies on a [single stabilizing gyroscope](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/hubble-one-gyro-mode/) .
