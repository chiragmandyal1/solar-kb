# APOD: 2025 October 12 - All the Water on Europa - NASA Science

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

**Explanation:** How much of [Jupiter's moon Europa](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap110130.html) is made of water? No one is sure, but probably a lot. Based on the Galileo probe data acquired during its [exploration of the Jovian system](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/galileo/) from 1995 to 2003, Europa possesses a deep, global ocean of liquid water beneath a layer of surface ice. [The subsurface ocean](http://www.universetoday.com/12143/%20europas-ocean-thick-or-thin/) plus ice layer could descend over 100 kilometers in average depth. Adopting a high-end estimate of 100 kilometers depth, if all the water on [Europa](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/europa/) were [gathered into a ball](https://media.istockphoto.com/id/1128004359/photo/close-up-scottish-fold-cat-head-with-shocking-face-and-wide-open-eyes-frighten-or-surprised.jpg?s=612x612&w=0&k=20&c=HglQ8Nf1PslTuI91T-dfhkln-iEbchjqfhGKCWTbaxg=) , it would have a radius of over 800 kilometers. To scale, this intriguing illustration compares that hypothetical ball of all the water on [Europa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa_\(moon\)) to the size of [Europa itself](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/pj-interior-of-europa/) (left) - and similarly to [all the water on planet Earth](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap250907.html) . With a volume possibly greater than Earth's oceans, the global subsurface ocean on Europa is a [tantalizing destination](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/) to search for extraterrestrial life in our Solar System. [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/)'s robotic [Europa Clipper](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/europa-clipper/) was [launched](https://youtu.be/uUpEEU7pplg) last year to investigate.
