# APOD: 2026 October 10 – Lunar Farside - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

[](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/apod/apod/2026/october/farside_lro1600.jpg)

**Explanation:** Tidally locked in [synchronous rotation](http://geosteph-adventuresinearthandspace.blogspot.com/2008/02/other-side-of-moon-synchronous-rotation.html), the [Moon always presents](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/educators/history) its familiar [nearside](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/?date=110303) to [denizens of planet Earth](https://www.artchive.com/artwork/a-sunday-afternoon-on-the-island-of-la-grande-jatte-georges-seurat-1884-1886/). From lunar orbit, the Moon's farside can become familiar, though. In fact [this sharp picture](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/298), a mosaic from the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter's wide angle camera, is centered on the lunar farside. Part of a [global mosaic](https://data.lroc.im-ldi.com/lroc/view_rdr/WAC_GLOBAL) of over 15,000 images acquired between November 2009 and February 2011, the highest resolution version shows features at a scale of 100 meters per pixel. Surprisingly, the rough and battered surface of the farside looks very different from the nearside covered with smooth dark lunar maria. A likely explanation is that the [farside crust is thicker](https://www.planetary.org/articles/the-two-faced-moon), making it harder for molten material from the interior to flow to the surface and form dark, [smooth maria](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/?date=260725).
