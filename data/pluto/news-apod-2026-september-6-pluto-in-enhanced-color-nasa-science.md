# APOD: 2026 September 6 – Pluto in Enhanced Color - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

[](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/apod/apod/2021/august/PlutoEnhancedHiRes_NewHorizons_5000.jpg)

**Explanation:** [Pluto](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/pluto/) is more colorful than we can see. Color data and high-resolution images of our Solar System's most famous [dwarf planet](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/), taken by the robotic [New Horizons spacecraft](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/new-horizons/) during its [flyby in 2015 July](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap150714.html), have been digitally combined to give an [enhanced-color view](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/rich-color-variations-of-pluto/) of this ancient world sporting an [unexpectedly young](https://www.reddit.com/r/cats/comments/lnfbzm/dear_smile_please_like_me/#lightbox) surface. The [featured enhanced color image](https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/the-rich-color-variations-of-pluto/) is not only [esthetically pretty](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap130401.html) but scientifically useful, making surface regions of differing chemical composition visually distinct. For example, the light-colored heart-shaped [Tombaugh Regio](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia19842-a-mountain-range-within-plutos-heart/) on the lower right is clearly shown here to be divisible into two regions that are [geologically different](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC7yXXCSR3_UB12gNWETiygA), with the leftmost lobe, [Sputnik Planitia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27905411/), also appearing unusually smooth. After Pluto, New Horizons continued on, shooting [past asteroid Arrokoth](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap191118.html) in 2019 with enough speed to escape our [Solar](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/) [System](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/menu/solar-system/) completely.
