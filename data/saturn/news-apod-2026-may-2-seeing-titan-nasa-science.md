# APOD: 2026 May 2 - Seeing Titan - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

**Explanation:** Shrouded in a thick atmosphere, the surface of [Saturn's largest moon, Titan](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/titan/facts/) , is really hard to see. Small particles suspended in Titan's upper atmosphere cause an almost impenetrable haze, strongly scattering light at visible wavelengths and hiding surface features from prying eyes. Still, Titan's [surface](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap150116.html) is better imaged at infrared wavelengths, where scattering is weaker and atmospheric absorption is reduced. Arrayed around this visible light image (center) of Titan are some of the clearest global infrared views of the [tantalizing](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap140919.html) moon so far. In false color, [the six panels present](https://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA21923) a consistent processing of 13 years of infrared image data from the Visual and Infrared Mapping Spectrometer (VIMS) on board [the Cassini spacecraft](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/) orbiting Saturn from 2004 to 2017. They offer a stunning comparison with Cassini's visible light view. [NASA's revolutionary rotorcraft](https://www.nasa.gov/dragonfly) mission to Titan's surface is due to launch no earlier than July, 2028.
