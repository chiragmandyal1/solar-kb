# APOD: 2025 October 5 - A Long Storm System on Saturn - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

[](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/apod/apod/2025/october/longstorm_cassini_960.jpg)

**Explanation:** It was one of the largest and longest lived storms ever recorded in our Solar System. First seen in late 2010, the featured cloud formation in the northern hemisphere of [Saturn](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/) started larger than the Earth and [soon spread](https://youtu.be/O7O8Hsuxjyo) completely around the planet. The storm was tracked not only [from Earth](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap110317.html) but from [up close](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap110119.html) by the robotic [Cassini spacecraft](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/spacecraft/cassini-orbiter/) then orbiting Saturn. [Pictured here](http://photojournal.jpl.nasa.gov/catalog/PIA12829) in false colored infrared in February, orange colors indicate [clouds deep](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap110708.html) in the atmosphere, while light colors highlight clouds higher up. The [rings of Saturn](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rings_of_Saturn) are seen nearly edge-on as the thin blue horizontal line. The warped dark bands are the [shadows of the rings](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap111012.html) cast onto the cloud tops by the Sun to the upper left. A source of radio noise from [lightning](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap100419.html) , the [intense storm](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap080505.html) was thought to relate to seasonal changes when spring [emerges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jW0EsR2FBD4) in the north of [Saturn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XvTSB5B0d0w) . After raging for over six months, the [iconic storm](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013Icar..223..460S/abstract) circled the entire planet and then tried to absorb its own tail -- which surprisingly caused it to [fade away](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/grand-finale/overview/) .
