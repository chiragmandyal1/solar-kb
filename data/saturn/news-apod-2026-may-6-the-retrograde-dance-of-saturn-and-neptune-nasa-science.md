# APOD: 2026 May 6 - The Retrograde Dance of Saturn and Neptune - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

**Explanation:** What does it mean for [Saturn](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/) and [Neptune](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/) to be in retrograde? Featured is a composite of images taken over 34 nights from May 2025 to February 2026 tracing Saturn (brighter, foreground) and Neptune (dimmer, background). Over that time, the two planets exhibited [retrograde motion](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap250530.html) , meaning they appeared to move backward in the sky. This [apparent backwards motion](https://youtu.be/1nVSzzYCAYk?si=I-42YO6rHQZ9CuNV&t=124) occurs when Earth overtakes the slower outer planets as they orbit the Sun. Imagine the Solar System is a running track. Earth “runs” faster along the inside of the track compared to the outer planets. As Earth approaches, aligns, and then “laps” the outer planets, they change position from ahead to behind from the Earth's perspective. This perspective shift is what causes the outer planets to change position in the night sky. [An animation](https://vimeo.com/1177973763) corresponding to today’s image shows Saturn and Neptune’s months-long dance across the northern night sky. Saturn stepped from [the Pisces constellation](https://noirlab.edu/public/education/constellations/pisces/) into [Aquarius](https://noirlab.edu/public/education/constellations/aquarius/) and back again while Neptune remained in Pisces. This is the closest Saturn and Neptune have been in the sky since their last [conjunction](https://earthsky.org/astronomy-essentials/what-is-a-conjunction/) in 1989.
