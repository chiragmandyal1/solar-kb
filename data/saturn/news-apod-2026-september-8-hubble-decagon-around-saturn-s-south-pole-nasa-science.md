# Hubble: Decagon Around Saturn's South Pole

**Explanation:** Why are Saturn’s poles geometric? [Saturn](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/)’s North Pole has been known to be surrounded by a [hexagonal](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap230618.html) (6 sides) cloud since [discovery in 1987](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1988Icar...76..335G/abstract) in data taken by NASA’s [Voyager spacecrafts](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/), which quickly flew past the [ringed world](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2020-april-19-cassini-approaches-saturn/) in the early 1980s. Now, recent observations of Saturn by the [Hubble Space Telescope](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap090525.html) reveal a slightly different geometric cloud pattern around the South Pole: a [decagon](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Decagon.html) (10 [sides](https://www.geogebra.org/m/bbD4R2Ck)). The geometric boundaries are [possibly caused](https://youtu.be/VQzLY17ncWM) by waves when the fast-moving gas away from the poles interacts with slower-moving gas closer to the poles. In the [featured image composite](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-tracks-new-decagon-encircling-saturns-south-pole/) by the Hubble taken last year, the [South Pole of Saturn](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap081027.html) is marked by an X and surrounded by bands of circulating clouds. The [decagon](https://science.nasa.gov/asset/hubble/decagon-on-saturns-south-pole-single-filter/) appears most prominent in the dark inner regions. The [northern hexagon](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap180907.html) has proven stable for over 40 years, while the stability of the southern decagon will surely remain a [topic of research](https://unsplash.com/photos/white-and-black-cat-on-white-textile-ZsMhJkloV64).
