# APOD: 2026 August 5 - Spokes on Saturn's B Ring - NASA Science

**Explanation:** Don’t get spooked by Saturn’s ghostly spokes! Today we feature a nearly two-hour timelapse of Saturn and its rings looping forwards and backwards. If you look closely, a ghoulish shadow appears and disappears as [Saturn’s B ring](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/saturns-rings-2/) rotates. Decades of observation with [Voyager 2](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/ring-spokes-4/), [Cassini](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2009-june-2-spokes-reappear-on-saturns-rings/), and [Hubble](https://science.nasa.gov/video-detail/hubble-saturn-oct2023-stsci-01hgxkq49bmac02hcazdvkea3f/) show the appearance of Saturn’s spokes varies with [the planet’s seasons](https://www.planetary.org/space-images/saturns-seasons). Like Earth, Saturn’s [spin axis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=46G1foTIivo) is tilted compared to the plane of its orbit around the Sun. During Saturn’s [equinox](https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/equinox/), the rings are [less tilted](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2025-november-16-crossing-saturns-ring-plane/) toward the Sun and they receive sunlight at a shallower angle. Saturn’s spokes may be electrically charged dust and ice temporarily levitated above the rings by [electromagnetic](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/overview/forces/#electromagnetic-force) forces. It is still uncertain, but the ring plasma and charge environment may be influenced by changing [ultraviolet](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/10_ultravioletwaves/) illumination, meteoroid impacts, or indirect effects from interactions between the [solar wind](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/nasas-hubble-watches-spoke-season-on-saturn/) and the planet’s magnetic field.
