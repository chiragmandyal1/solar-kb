# APOD: 2026 August 11 - Six Moons of Saturn - NASA Science

**Explanation:** [How many moons does Saturn have?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moons_of_Saturn) While the total will likely continue to grow, as of June 2026 the ringed gas giant had 293 confirmed moons. That's easily more [than any other planet](http://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/?sat_discovery) of the Solar System, including ruling gas giant Jupiter with a mere 115 confirmed moons. Most of Saturn's known moons are small, irregular satellites. Many are only few kilometers to a fraction of a kilometer across and [grouped in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moons_of_Saturn#Irregular_moons) tilted outer orbits. Six of its largest satellites can be seen here, though, in this sharp telescopic [Saturnian family portrait](https://app.astrobin.com/i/ztmhhp) taken on August 5. Larger than Earth's Moon and even slightly larger than inner planet Mercury, [Titan](http://solarsystem.nasa.gov/planets/profile.cfm?Object=Sat_Titan), with a diameter of 5,150 kilometers, is at lower right. You can also spot icy major moons [Mimas](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap100511.html), [Tethys](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap091208.html), [Enceladus](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap120208.html), [Dione](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap070801.html), and [Rhea](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap080513.html) in the frame. Saturn's first known natural satellite, Titan was [discovered in 1655](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/fap/ap050325.html) by Dutch astronomer Christiaan Huygens. During the space age Voyager and [Cassini discoveries](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/science/moons/) have added to the [swelling ranks](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025PSJ.....6..283A/abstract) of Saturnian moons.
