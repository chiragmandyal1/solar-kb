# APOD: 2026 April 30 - Waves on Titan - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

**Explanation:** Have you ever thought about surfing on an alien world? We can now expand the search for the [perfect wave](https://www.popsci.com/environment/whats-the-formula-for-the-perfect-wave/) from Earth to the rest of the Solar System, and [beyond](https://cdn.stocksnap.io/img-thumbs/960w/surfboard-beach_7A561BC965.jpg) . Scientists have developed a new model for simulating [waves](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/wavesinocean.html) on [other planets](https://news.mit.edu/2026/waves-hit-different-on-other-planets-0416) . [Titan](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/titan/) is one of the 274 confirmed [moons](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap210706.html) of [Saturn](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/) to date, and the only object in the solar system (besides Earth) known to have liquid lakes and seas on its surface. The [featured video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6kECVsTTetM&t=2s) shows a simulation of waves on Earth (right) and on Titan (left), under the same conditions (the scale marker is in meters). A light breeze would create taller, slower-moving waves on Titan than on Earth, because the [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakes_and_rivers_of_Titan) there are filled with light liquid hydrocarbons , and because of Titan's low gravity and higher atmospheric pressure. In a couple of years, [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/) expects to launch the [Dragonfly](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/dragonfly/) mission, which will travel for 6 years and send a [rotorcraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotorcraft) to explore Titan and study its [microbial habitability](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/the-habitability-of-titan-and-its-ocean/) .
