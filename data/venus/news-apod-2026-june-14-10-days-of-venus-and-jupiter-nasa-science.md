# APOD: 2026 June 14 - 10 Days of Venus and Jupiter - NASA Science

APOD

Astronomy Picture of the Day

[Discover the cosmos!](https://science.nasa.gov/apod/archive/) Each day a different image or photograph of our fascinating universe is featured, along with a brief explanation written by a professional astronomer.

[](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/cds/apod/apod/2026/june/VenusJupiter10_Pawar_1080.jpg)

**Explanation:** [Venus and Jupiter](https://earthsky.org/tonight/venus-jupiter-conjunction-june-2026-charts-how-to-see/) may have caught your attention lately. The [recent close conjunction](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap260612.html) of the two brightest planets in recent evening skies has been [hard to miss](https://www.reddit.com/media?url=https%3A%2F%2Fi.redd.it%2F56kpgyhj47dg1.png) . With Jupiter at the top, starting on May 30 and ending on June 8, their close approach was chronicled daily, left to right, in the [featured panels](https://www.instagram.com/p/DZVJqllozdB/) from [Maharashtra](https://youtu.be/oHVFNF11y9Q) , [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India) . Near the western horizon, the [evening sky colors](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap130314.html) and exposures used for each panel depend on the local conditions near sunset. At their closest on June 9, the [celestial pair](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap260607.html) appeared to be only about three times the width of a full moon apart. Of course, on that date, the [two planets](https://www.facebook.com/media/set/?set=a.996452506416660&type=3) were physically separated by over 600 million kilometers in their [orbits around the Sun](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/) . In the coming days, [Jupiter](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap250216.html) will slowly settle into the sunset glare, but [Venus](https://science.nasa.gov/venus/) will continue to move farther from the Sun in the western sky to excel in its current role as the [brilliant evening star](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/whats-up-june-2026-skywatching-tips-from-nasa/) .
