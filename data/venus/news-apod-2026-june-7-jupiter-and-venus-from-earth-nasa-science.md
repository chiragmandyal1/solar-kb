# Jupiter and Venus from Earth

**Explanation:** It was visible around the world. The sunset conjunction of Jupiter (left) and Venus (right) in 2012 was visible almost [no matter where you lived](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap120307.html) on Earth. Anyone on [our planet](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/) with a clear western horizon at sunset could see them. That year, a creative photographer traveled away from the town lights of [Szubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szubin) , [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland) to photograph a near closest approach of the [two planets](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8br4JiFEik) . The [bright planets](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap120316.html) were then separated by only [three degrees](http://www.universetoday.com/94113/venus-jupiter-conjunction-march-15th-2012/) and his daughter struck a [humorous pose](https://i.pinimg.com/originals/03/f3/e5/03f3e504f99ee70a9b29dd472be70483.jpg) . A faint [red sunset](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap250615.html) still glowed in the background. [Jupiter](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/) and [Venus](https://science.nasa.gov/venus/) are [together again](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/news/venus-jupiter-25-31-may-2026) this week after sunset, [passing within a degree](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap230304.html) of each other about [two days from today](https://earthsky.org/tonight/venus-jupiter-conjunction-june-2026-charts-how-to-see/) .
